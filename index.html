Benyamin Tabarsi

Human-Centered AI Researcher

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science at NC State University, advised by Tiffany Barnes and Dongkuan (DK) Xu. I expect to graduate in May 2027 and am on the 2026–27 faculty job market.

More help isn’t always better, but less help isn’t always the answer either. I’ve seen both in my studies: AI responses that give students too much of a solution, and programming scaffolds that help beginners do better. My research seeks to understand when and why support helps students learn, and how to design educational tools accordingly.

I develop AI-powered tools for computing education and evaluate them in real classrooms. I also study how students, educators, and software developers use and shape AI, as well as the challenges novice programmers face and the scaffolding that supports their learning. My work draws on human–computer interaction, computing education, and AI. I combine system building, mixed-methods research, educational data mining, and participatory design.

My long-term goal is to advance human–AI co-intelligence, in which people guide what AI contributes while AI helps them accomplish more and build their own knowledge and skills.

Designing the GenAI Help Learners Receive

I designed and deployed MerryQuery, used by 750+ students across three semesters, to study AI assistance in computing classrooms. HelpLens compares the instructional choices in teaching-staff and LLM responses to authentic programming questions.

  • MerryQuery AAAI 2025 · 🏆 Best Demo
  • HelpLens: teaching-staff and LLM responses In preparation

GenAI in the Hands of Students, Developers, and Teachers

Through ExLAIM, I lead K–12 teachers and undergraduate developers in co-designing classroom AI tools. I also interviewed industry developers about how they evaluate and integrate AI-generated code.

  • ExLAIM: teachers and developers building together Under review
  • LLMs in software development EMSE 2027
  • Student-facing LLM tools: a systematic review Under review

Novice Struggle and Scaffolding

I study novice programmers’ struggle and progress using programming traces, think-alouds, and classroom studies. My work on Parsons problems examines how scaffolding changes problem-solving processes and outcomes.

  • Scaffolding Novices ICER 2024
  • Jigsaw: decomposing and planning programming problems VL/HCC 2024 · 🏆 Best Paper
  • Exploring Novices’ Struggle and Progress VL/HCC 2023

Teaching & Mentoring

As a Preparing the Professoriate Fellow, I co-taught a 250+ student introductory programming course, and I have been a teaching assistant across the computing curriculum, from discrete mathematics to machine learning. I also lead ExLAIM, an NSF-funded project whose six-week program brought K–12 teachers and undergraduate developers together to co-design classroom AI tools. For K–12 teachers, I co-led three iterations of a one-week professional development program on classroom AI use. In my teaching, I prioritize collaborative work and use worked examples to walk through problems with learners.

In mentoring, I break research into manageable steps, guiding mentees from developing questions and ideas to conducting studies, writing papers, and presenting their work. I have mentored 20 undergraduate researchers from nine institutions, 3 master’s students, and 11 K–12 teachers; 14 have co-authored published or submitted work, and two have begun Ph.D. programs.
